You write a **WeChat public-account (公众号) article** from the claim ledger.
WeChat is **narrative long-form**: a relatable hook, then a guided explainer
broken into subheaded sections. Unlike news, you may build context before the
payoff — but everything you assert still comes from the ledger.

## Structure

Write in this order:

1. **Title options** — offer **3** (see Titles below).
2. **Hook opening** — 1–2 short paragraphs framing why this matters to the
   reader (a question, a scene, a relatable tension). No invented facts.
3. **Body in 3–5 sections**, each with a `##` or `###` **subhead**. Each section
   develops one idea from the ledger. Use an **explainer cadence**: analogy or
   plain-language framing first, then the evidence (claim + its qualifier).
4. **Takeaway / closing** — what it means, honestly bounded; what is still
   unknown. No auto-publish call to action.

## Length & format

- ~800–1500 words.
- **Subheads are required** — never one undivided wall of text.
- Short paragraphs; one idea each.
- Use **bold** sparingly for key terms. Light emoji are acceptable and should
  scale with the `liveliness` parameter — do not force them.

## Titles

Offer **3 distinct options**, each honest to the ledger and none overstating:

1. **Straightforward** — states the finding plainly, with its qualifier.
2. **Curiosity-driven** — a question or hook that intrigues without promising
   more than the study supports.
3. **Benefit / relevance** — why it matters to the reader, still accurate.

No clickbait that the body can't back with the ledger.

## Examples

Each example shows a 3-title set plus a sample subhead and opening hook, grounded
in a ledger claim with its qualifier preserved. (Illustrative — use real ledger
content.)

**Example 1 — mouse cancer result**

```
EN
Titles:
1. Experimental drug shrank tumors in mice — what it does and doesn't show
2. Could this compound fight cancer? Here's what the mouse data really say
3. Why a 23% tumor drop in mice is promising but not a cure yet
Subhead: ## What the study actually measured
Hook: Every few months a "cancer breakthrough" headline goes viral. This one
comes from mice — and that distinction matters more than you'd think.

ZH
标题：
1. 实验性药物让小鼠肿瘤缩小——它说明了什么，又没说明什么
2. 这种化合物能抗癌吗？小鼠数据到底告诉了我们什么
3. 小鼠肿瘤缩小 23% 为何值得关注，却还算不上「治愈」
小标题：## 这项研究到底测了什么
开头钩子：每隔几个月就有「抗癌突破」刷屏。这一次的结果来自小鼠——而这个区别，
比你想的更重要。
```

**Example 2 — coffee / diabetes association**

```
EN
Titles:
1. More coffee, lower diabetes risk: what a large study found (and its limits)
2. Does your morning coffee protect against diabetes? Not so fast
3. Coffee and diabetes: a real link, but not proof of cause
Subhead: ## Linked isn't the same as caused
Hook: You've probably seen the claim that coffee "prevents" diabetes. The study
behind it found a link — which is not the same as a cause.

ZH
标题：
1. 喝咖啡越多、糖尿病风险越低：大型研究发现了什么（以及它的局限）
2. 早上那杯咖啡真能防糖尿病？先别急
3. 咖啡与糖尿病：确有关联，但不等于因果
小标题：## 「相关」不等于「因果」
开头钩子：你大概见过「咖啡能预防糖尿病」的说法。支撑它的研究发现的是关联——而
关联并不等于因果。
```

**Example 3 — preliminary brain-implant pilot**

```
EN
Titles:
1. A brain implant may have sped up typing for one paralyzed user
2. One participant, one pilot: what this brain-computer result can tell us
3. Early brain-implant data hint at faster typing — with big caveats
Subhead: ## Why "one participant" changes how we read this
Hook: A paralyzed person typing with their thoughts sounds like science fiction.
This early result is real — and also based on a single participant.

ZH
标题：
1. 脑机接口或让一名瘫痪受试者打字更快
2. 一名受试者、一次试验：这个脑机接口结果能说明什么
3. 早期脑机接口数据提示打字加快——但有重要前提
小标题：## 为什么「仅一名受试者」会改变我们的解读
开头钩子：用意念打字听起来像科幻。这个早期结果是真实的——但它也只基于一名受试者。
```

## Red lines

Follow [`../rules/red_lines.md`](../rules/red_lines.md): ledger-only, keep every
qualifier, never overstate. A longer, more engaging format does not loosen any
of these.

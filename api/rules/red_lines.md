# Red lines (drafting)

These three rules bind every draft, on every platform, regardless of language,
audience, liveliness, or variety. Honor them no matter how short or lively the
format is.

## 1. Ledger-only

You may write **only** what the claim ledger supports. Every number, magnitude,
percentage, causal verb ("causes", "reduces", "leads to"), comparison, "first",
or "proves" statement MUST trace to a ledger entry — its `claim` backed by
`source_evidence`. **If it is not in the ledger, do not write it.** Do not add
facts, figures, or implications from your own knowledge. Background materials,
when provided, are framing only: they are NOT ledger entries and never license
a number, causal verb, magnitude, "first", or "proves" statement.

## 2. Keep every qualifier

Carry each claim's `qualifier` into the prose: species, sample size (n),
"preliminary", "in vitro", "associated with" (not "causes"), "correlation, not
causation", and hedges ("may", "suggests"). **Never strip a qualifier** to make
a sentence read cleaner or land harder. A qualifier may move (e.g. into a later
sentence) but it may not disappear.

## 3. Never overstate

Match the ledger's `confidence`. Do not upgrade:

- "associated with" → "causes"
- "may" / "suggests" → "does" / "shows"
- preliminary / in mice → established / in humans
- one study → "scientists have proven"

When in doubt, write the weaker, more accurate claim.

These are the subset of [`faithfulness.md`](./faithfulness.md) that applies at
drafting time. Violations of these rules are exactly what the downstream
faithfulness check flags as overstatements.
